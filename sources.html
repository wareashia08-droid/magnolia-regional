Brand and design choices

3 topics

Magnolia Regional Medical Center is a fictional hospital, so its whole identity was designed for this project. These are the reasons behind its three biggest choices.

Why a magnolia

  • It feels local. The southern magnolia is one of the best-known trees of the American South, including Georgia, where the hospital is set. A regional hospital should feel like it belongs to its community.
  • It matches the message. Magnolias are long-lived and come back into bloom year after year. Magnolias are also one of the oldest groups of flowering plants on Earth. That steadiness supports the tagline “Care, nurtured to last.”
  • It feels welcoming, not clinical. Most hospital logos use a cross, a heart or an abstract shape. A flower gives the brand a softer, more personal face, which suits a place people often visit when they are worried.

Why plant-inspired colors

Every color in the palette comes from the magnolia itself, so the logo, photos and page all feel like one brand.

  • Forest green #27483d
    From: The magnolia’s glossy leaves
    Used for: Headings and main buttons
  • Deep forest #18352d
    From: Shaded undersides of the leaves
    Used for: Top bar and footer
  • Ivory #fffaf1
    From: The white magnolia bloom
    Used for: Page background
  • Blush #f4e2e1
    From: Soft pink petal edges
    Used for: Highlights and the patient story
  • Mint #e7eee4
    From: New spring leaves
    Used for: Calm section backgrounds and “Open now” labels
  • Plum #8347ae
    From: Purple tulip magnolia buds
    Used for: Links and small accents
  • Deep rose #9b2c3f
    From: The darkest petal tones
    Used for: Saved for emergencies and errors
  • Calm instead of cold. Greens and soft natural tones are often used in healthcare spaces because they feel calm and restorative. They also set Magnolia Regional apart from the usual hospital blue and white.
  • Red is saved for emergencies. Because the rest of the palette is soft, the deep rose used for emergency information stands out right away.
  • Readable for everyone. Every text color was checked against its background and meets the WCAG AA contrast guideline (at least 4.5 to 1), so the soft palette never makes text hard to read.

Why this photo of the hospital

  • The building grows plants. Its terraced balconies are filled with greenery, which echoes the plant-inspired palette and the idea of care that grows with you.
  • It feels hopeful. Bright daylight and a clear blue sky make the hospital look open and optimistic rather than intimidating.
  • It shows a connected campus. The covered walkway, with people crossing it, suggests a place that is easy to get around for patients and visitors.
  • It lets the logo lead. The photo has no text built into it, so the site’s own logo and headline stay the main brand elements.

The photo shows a real building in Singapore, used here to represent the fictional hospital. It is credited in the Unsplash photos section below.

Fictional content

Note

Magnolia Regional Medical Center is not a real hospital. The following were invented for this assignment and are not drawn from any source:

  • The hospital name, address, phone numbers and email
  • Clinician names, profiles, languages and availability, the patient story, events, hours and the insurance list. The doctor photos show stock models, not real staff.
  • The campus map, which is an original illustration drawn in code
  • The services, hours and emergency department details, which are modeled on real hospitals but describe no real facility

Magnolia Regional is not affiliated with Wellstar Health System. Health guidance on the page, such as calling 911 in an emergency, is general public advice.
